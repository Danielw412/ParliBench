You are preparing the Government side for a high school PHSSL Parliamentary Debate round.

Motion:
{{MOTION}}

Your goal is to produce the strongest useful Government preparation for skilled debaters.

PHSSL Parliamentary Debate emphasizes logical reasoning, persuasive argumentation, general knowledge, useful examples, and comparative analysis rather than relying too much on published sources.

Before answering, consider a broad range of plausible Government arguments. Discard arguments that are weak, repetitive, highly speculative, easily rebutted, or unnecessarily complicated. Do not output the discarded arguments.

Output exactly the 3-4 strongest Government contentions.

## Motion Interpretation

First determine what the motion is actually asking Government to establish.

Adapt your case to the type of motion:

- For a POLICY or ACTION motion, explain what Government supports and the important mechanism of the change. Do not over-specify an unnecessary implementation plan.
- For a VALUE or MORAL motion, establish the principle or standard that should decide the debate and explain its practical consequences.
- For a FACTUAL or PREDICTIVE motion, establish why the proposition is likely or true. Do not turn it into an unrelated policy proposal.
- For a REGRETS motion, compare the real world with the plausible counterfactual in which the regretted development, decision, or trend did not occur. Government must show why that counterfactual is better overall.
- For an ABSTRACT, METAPHORICAL, or IDIOMATIC motion, choose the most reasonable and educational interpretation of the wording. Briefly state that interpretation before arguing it. Do not exploit ambiguity to create an artificially easy case.

Do not redefine a motion merely to defend an easier subset of what it actually asks.

"This House" may only be defined as:
- the people in the room
- the United States Federal Government
- the United Nations
- a non-specific developing country
- the state of Pennsylvania
- a non-specific local government
- another House made obvious by the wording of the resolution

If the House is made obvious by the motion, use that actor.

Keep framing and definitions brief. Only define terms that materially affect the debate.

Do not use abusive definitions, technicalities, or interpretations that avoid the central controversy.

For EACH contention, use this structure:

## Contention [number]: [short descriptive title]

### Claim

State the central claim in roughly one sentence.

Use simple, clear language that a debater could easily understand and explain during the round.

### Warrants

Explain why the claim is true.

Use multiple distinct warrants where useful. Warrants may include:

Logic-driven warrant:
Explain the causal chain step by step. Show why one event, incentive, belief, or behavior leads to the next.

Example-driven warrant:
Use a relevant real-world, historical, current-event, hypothetical, or intuitive example that demonstrates or illustrates the reasoning.

Evidence/trend-driven warrant:
Use a well-established fact, pattern, trend, or statistic when useful

You do not need to use every type of warrant.

Never invent statistics, studies, quotations, events, or factual details.

Distinguish between:
- what an example actually demonstrates, and
- the broader conclusion you infer from it.

### Impact

Explain the terminal consequence of the argument.

Do not stop at an intermediate impact such as:
"this hurts the economy,"
"this reduces trust,"
"this creates instability,"
or
"this violates rights."

Explain why that outcome ultimately matters to people, institutions, society, or the world.

Trace the impact to the final meaningful consequence.

For primarily moral or philosophical motions, explain both why the principle matters intrinsically and, where relevant, what accepting or rejecting it means in practice.

### Comparative

Explain why this contention helps Government win the motion rather than merely establishing that Government produces some benefit.

Compare Government with the strongest plausible Opposition world, interpretation, or counterfactual.

If the contention relies on an alternative system or counterfactual, explain:
- why that alternative is realistically plausible, and
- why it avoids the identified harm rather than recreating the same problem in another form.

Explain why Government's advantage is more likely, larger, more important, more durable, or otherwise outweighs the competing consideration.

### Likely Opposition response

Briefly identify the strongest realistic response Opposition could make to this contention.

Do not choose an intentionally weak response.

### Government defense

Briefly explain the strongest answer Government should give.

Do not merely repeat the original contention. Explain why the Opposition response fails, is comparatively less important, or can be conceded without losing the contention.

After all contentions, include:

## Round Priorities

Identify the 2–3 most important propositions Government must establish to win the debate.

Explain which clashes deserve the most attention and how Government should weigh them.

GENERAL REQUIREMENTS:

- Prioritize depth over quantity.
- Prefer clear causal reasoning over assertions.
- Prefer contentions that remain strong after obvious Opposition responses.
- Use concrete examples when they materially improve the argument.
- Include non-obvious arguments when genuinely useful, but do not sacrifice plausibility for creativity.
- Do not force consequential or policy analysis onto a motion that is primarily moral, philosophical, factual, or abstract.
- Distinguish factual background from your inference about what that fact means.
- Write concise prep notes, not a polished speech.
- Do not include citations, URLs, a bibliography, or a sources section.
- Stay within 900 words.
